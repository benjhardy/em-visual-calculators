Skin effect

AC current crowds toward the surface of a conductor. Move the frequency and watch where it flows.

Material

AC resistance over DC resistance

Exact Bessel solution against the thin-skin approximations.

Skin depth

δ = √(2ρ / ωµ). Where δ drops below the radius, the skin takes over.

Check

Method

δ = √(2ρ / (ω µ0 µr)) k = (1 − j) / δ J(r) = J(0) · J0(k r) (exact, round wire) Z/Rdc = (k a / 2) · J0(ka) / J1(ka) Rac/Rdc = Re{Z/Rdc} thin skin: Rac/Rdc ≈ a/(2δ) + 1/4 flat: J(z) = J(0) · e^(−z/δ) · e^(−j z/δ)

J0 and J1 of complex argument: power series for |ka| ≤ 25, Hankel asymptotic expansion above (scaled, so very large a/δ does not overflow).

Reference values · q = √2·a/δ

Columns: this page's Bessel solution; an independent Kelvin-function (ber/bei) series, Rac/Rdc = (q/2)(ber·bei′ − bei·ber′)/(ber′² + bei′²); classic tabulated values; and the asymptote a/(2δ) + 1/4 + 3δ/(32a) for large q.
